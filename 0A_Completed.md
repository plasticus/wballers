## Phase 0 — Foundation and product definition

- **App scaffolding.** Flutter project (`womensbballmgr`), Android-first with a Linux desktop target for development. Feature-module structure, Riverpod for state management and dependency injection, `dart format`/`flutter analyze`/`flutter test` as the standing quality gate before every commit. CI runs formatting, static analysis, unit tests, and Android builds.
- **Local persistence.** `SaveRepository` is the storage-agnostic boundary; `FileSaveRepository` is the real Android/desktop implementation (temp-file-then-rename writes, so a crash mid-write can't corrupt a save). `SaveEnvelope` wraps every payload with a `schemaVersion`. Originally exactly one save slot with no picker UI -- see the Settings-screen entry near the end of this doc (2026-08-07) for the real 3-slot picker that superseded that.
- **Design foundations.** Material 3 theme (light/dark, one navy/gold identity), a coach-controlled text-scale multiplier layered on top of the OS setting (important: the user has low vision and needs generous, adjustable text), a five-step spacing scale, and shared components (`AppCard`, `LoadingView`/`EmptyStateView`/`ErrorStateView`, `AdPlacementPlaceholder`). Accessibility rules established and enforced throughout: never hardcode font sizes against the ambient scale, label icon-only/decorative elements for screen readers, never rely on color alone to convey status, never clip text that scales.
- **Ad placement reserved.** `AdPlacementPlaceholder` reserves dashboard layout space for a future AdMob banner — no SDK integration yet, that's Phase 5.
- **League team pool.** 40 fictional teams (`teams.md`, `kLeagueTeamPool`), 20 each in the Atlantic and Pacific conferences (including four Mexican clubs: Mérida and Monterrey in Atlantic, Tijuana and Juárez in Pacific), with names/colors/identity notes designed to evoke real professional women's basketball without mirroring any actual team. `drawLeagueTeams` (`league_draw.dart`) randomly draws 10 per conference into a real playthrough's league, deterministic from the franchise's `simulationSeed` so the same save always reconstructs the same 20-team league on reload — so which teams actually exist varies game to game instead of always being the same fixed 20.
- **Fictional college pipeline.** 100 fictional colleges (`colleges.md`) across real-ish U.S./Canadian regions, each with a prestige tier (Premier/Strong/Developing) that will eventually flavor the draft pipeline.
- **Portrait asset audit.** The existing layered pixel-art system (LibreSprite-drawn, 32×32, no third-party art) was cataloged and a Flutter migration spec written (`portraits.md`) — full build-out landed in Phase 1.5, below.

## Phase 1 — Expansion franchise, players, and rosters

- **Player identity and ratings.** Fictional name, age, years of service (tracked independently of age — an international player can debut as a rookie in their late twenties), hometown, position(s), handedness, biography, and standing height in inches (`heightInches`, 5'2"-7'0", generated per-position with enough jitter that real outliers happen — a tall point guard, a shorter center — rather than height being implied by position alone). Twelve stored ratings on a shared 1-99 scale (0 and 100 deliberately excluded — a rating is never "nothing" or "perfect"): four physical (speed, agility, strength, stamina), four offensive (ball control, passing, interior offense, perimeter offense), four defensive/playmaking (perimeter defense, interior defense, disruption, blocking), plus `potential` as a separate ceiling rating. In-game action success will use one universal formula at simulation time — Physical Stat + Skill/Defensive Stat — rather than pre-computed derived stats; rebounding specifically isn't stored at all, it's that formula applied twice (strength + interior offense for an offensive rebound, strength + interior defense for a defensive one).
- **The star-rating system.** Replaces a salary cap entirely (`star_system.md`). 1-99 OVR maps to stars: 5-star (90-99), 4-star (78-89), 3-star-and-below (1-77). A 12-player active roster is governed by two nested caps, not fixed recipes: at most 2 five-star players, and at most 6 players total who are four-star-or-better (so a team with zero five-stars can still carry six four-stars — the cap doesn't shrink just because no five-star slots are used). Limits lock only at the regular-season buzzer; a mid-season breakout can temporarily overshoot, but the roster must be legal again before free agency and the draft.
- **Roster statuses**, loosely mirroring the real WNBA CBA without replicating its finer mechanics (`wnba_rules_reference.md`): **Active** (star-tier caps apply, no enforced minimum size), **Developmental** (at most 2, exempt from star caps, restricted to ≤3 years of service), **Reserve/Inactive** (unconstrained catch-all, not split into injury/suspension/hardship sub-categories since none of those distinctions currently affect anything).
- **Seeded, deterministic generation.** `generatePlayer` draws name/hometown from invented pools, rolls age 20-34 with years-of-service derived from a separately-rolled debut age (19-28), and builds ratings from a quality center plus per-stat jitter plus a per-position bias table (centers skew strength/interior up and speed/perimeter down, etc.) so generated rosters read as basketball players, not clones with a label. `generateStartingRoster` composes this into a new expansion franchise's 12-player roster with guaranteed full position coverage, meaningfully different seed to seed. (Originally shipped deliberately weak and structurally incapable of reaching the four-star threshold at all -- superseded by the team-overall-rebalance entry in Phase 2 below, which gives it a real narrative core of four hand-placed players instead.)
- **The GM is not the coach.** The human player creates a General Manager persona (name only); the coach is a generated NPC hire with their own stat block (`CoachStats`: Offense, Defense, Development, Motivation, Management — deliberately much smaller than player ratings, defined ahead of the Phase 2/3 systems that will consume them, the same way player ratings were defined before simulation existed to use them).
- **Starting lineup.** One player per position, defaulting at franchise creation to the best-available eligible active player (greedy, not a global optimum — the GM is expected to adjust it), editable via a dedicated screen. Legality checking (all positions filled, no double-booked player, every starter active and eligible) surfaces which specific rule failed rather than a bare pass/fail.
- **Expansion onboarding.** The GM names themselves and the club, picks a home city and conference, and gets a generated coach plus a weak generated roster. Onboarding draws this playthrough's 20-team league up front (real entropy, once, in `initState` — see `drawLeagueTeams`) so the conference's 10 drawn teams show as a checkbox list — one pre-checked at random (re-rolled on conference switch), captioned "Choose the team to replace," GM-overridable. The choice is recorded (`Franchise.replacedTeamAbbreviation`) and consumed by `LeagueScreen` (below), which substitutes the GM's own club in for the replaced team.
- **Save-game schema.** `Franchise` (the save-game root: GM name, team, coach, roster, simulation seed, replaced-team choice) round-trips through manual JSON (de)serialization per feature and `SaveEnvelope`/`SaveRepository`, proven end-to-end including a real full-app-restart simulation in tests. `Player` carries a stable `id` so lineup slots and other cross-references survive a save/reload, where object identity and list position are both lost.
- **Roster screens.** `TeamRosterScreen` (the Team tab): roster grouped by status, sorted by position then overall, starters marked (the top 5 in bench order -- see the team-overall-rebalance/bench-order-drives-minutes entry below), portraits and archetypes shown, subtle per-position color accents. Each row also shows height (`formatHeightInches`) alongside archetype/age, and offense/defense/physical overall summaries (`PlayerRatings.offenseOverall`/`defenseOverall`/`physicalOverall`, unweighted averages of their four underlying ratings, same shape as `overall`) beneath that. `DepthChartScreen` for the bench: drag-reorder the full active roster into a minutes-ranked order, with each rank's `targetMinutesForRank` shown as an informational note (the Phase 3 Target Minutes reference table) — developmental players show separately at a fixed 0 minutes. (Originally shipped alongside a separate `LineupEditorScreen` for a position-locked starting five and "nothing consumes this ordering yet" -- both superseded, see below.)
- **League screen shows the effective league, not just the static pool.** Once a franchise exists, `LeagueScreen` re-derives that save's drawn 20-team league (same `drawLeagueTeams` call, same `simulationSeed`) and substitutes the GM's own club in for whichever drawn team it replaced (badged "Your Team"), so the listing genuinely reads as 19 AI teams + 1 GM team instead of 21 clubs. Before a franchise exists there's no league to derive, so the screen prompts to create one instead of guessing. The other 19 teams now have real generated rosters behind them too — see Phase 2, below.

## Phase 1.5 — Portraits and earned identity

- **Traits.** A 29-entry catalog (`traits.md`) of discrete, visible, earnable personality/career/skill-badge traits, replacing an earlier hidden-numeric-stat idea outright — a coach should be able to scout for something like Leadership on the trade market, not have it be an invisible number. Built: the `Trait` enum, mutually-exclusive opposite pairs (Clutch/Choker, Leader/Malcontent, etc., enforced so a player can never carry both sides). `TraitCategory` groups the 29 into the 7 sections `traits.md` already organizes them into (work ethic, durability, leadership, mental, loyalty, crowd, skill badges). Displayed on the roster row as small rounded-corner chips naming each trait, tinted by category — decorative color only, the trait name text always carries the actual information.
- **Trait distribution is team-wide, not per player.** Reframed after the original per-player 0-3 roll made every 12-player roster read as trait-saturated (~18 total on average) instead of special. `distributeTraits` (`roster/generation/trait_distribution.dart`) now assigns 3-5 traits total across a freshly generated roster, almost always one each to different players -- a player getting a second, different trait is a deliberate rarity (~5% chance per roster). `generatePlayer` no longer rolls traits itself; `generateStartingRoster` and `generateAiRoster` both call `distributeTraits` once all 12 players exist. The old per-player `generateTraits` (`player/generation/trait_generator.dart`) is kept, unused for now, for the eventual draft-prospect generator, where rolling a trait or two for one incoming player at a time is the right shape again -- most traits are meant to arrive that way going forward, or later through a small aging/development chance, neither built yet.
- **Archetypes.** A position-keyed play-style catalog (`archetypes.md`, 15 unique labels across the 5 positions, several shared across multiple positions). Built: the `Archetype` enum and position-eligibility map, with random-per-position generation. Rating-correlated selection (a "Sniper" should actually have high perimeter offense) is still open — see `0B_Planned.md`.
- **Portrait rendering engine.** Full pipeline from the original layered pixel-art system (`portraits.md`) ported to Flutter: `PortraitAppearance` (source data — skin tone, hair style/color, eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth, accessories, plus coach-only shoulders/hats/glasses/facial hair), seeded generation from the real bundled `weights.json`/`manifest.json`, a pixel-for-pixel `dart:ui` port of the reference recoloring math (magenta-placeholder hair/eyebrow recolor, exact-match base-sprite skin/shadow/jersey recolor, nose-shadow recolor), and a compositor producing cached PNGs. Every generation function takes portrait data as an *optional* parameter, so it can be safely omitted without disturbing existing determinism.
- **Portrait height-shift by actual height, not position.** Reframed from the original position-based idea: driven by `Player.heightInches`, not a blanket per-position rule, so a player's portrait matches their real generated height including outliers (a tall point guard, a shorter center). A naive whole-image shift was prototyped and rejected first — it just clipped the hair and left a gap at the bottom, didn't read as "taller." Built instead: three hand-drawn base-sprite variants in `base/` (`BlankBaldwoman32H0/H1/H2.png` — H0 also matches `player-edit.html`'s `window.BASE_SPRITE`), where the taller variants bake the extra height into the body/shoulders art directly, no shift needed there. `PortraitHeightTier.of(player)` classifies `heightInches` into baseline/tall/tallest (cutoffs at 6'0" and 6'4", tuned against the generator's real output to land close to a 40/40/20 split with the tallest tier the rarest). The renderer selects the matching base sprite and shifts every other layer (hair, eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth, accessories) up 1px/2px to realign with the taller sprite's higher head position — a coach's shoulders stay unshifted, anchored to the body, same as the base sprite (coaches have no height stat, so always render baseline). Verified by rendering real generated portraits at each tier and inspecting them directly, not just by test assertions.
- **Portrait caching.** Rendered PNGs are cached separately from save data (derived, rebuildable, never authoritative), keyed on appearance version and jersey color so a trade or an edit can never serve a stale image.
- **Portrait editor.** Every field the design doc calls for — skin tone, hair, eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth, accessories for everyone; shoulders, hats, glasses, facial hair additionally for the coach — with a live, un-cached preview. Reachable from a roster row's portrait or the coach row above the roster. A coach always gets shoulders and never gets goggles specifically (confirmed against the original portrait-creator tool's own behavior and diagnostics, which `weights.json` alone didn't capture).
- **Achievement/nickname data model.** `Achievement` enum (league MVP, scoring leader, defensive MVP, most defensive disruptions), `PlayerAchievementRecord`, a curated nickname suggestion pool per award type, and the special/neon hair-color unlock gate in the portrait editor (only appears once a player has earned at least one achievement). This is infrastructure only — nothing in the running app can actually grant an achievement yet, since that needs Phase 2's season simulation to determine a winner. Nicknames are deliberately *not* a free GM-assignment tool; an early pass got this wrong and it was corrected.
- **Roster row polish.** Nickname shown in quotes when present; the unhelpful "3★ & below" star-tier label was dropped; position abbreviations got a subtle color accent (decorative only, the abbreviation text still carries the actual information).

## Phase 2 — League, season, and franchise simulation

- **The 19 AI teams have real, persisted rosters.** `League`/`AiTeamRoster` (`league/domain/`) hold the other 19 teams in this playthrough's drawn league (everyone except the GM's own club) plus each one's actual 12-player roster — no longer identity-only. `generateLeague` builds it once at franchise creation from the same `simulationSeed` that drives the league draw and the GM's own starting roster (a distinct seed offset, `kLeagueRosterSeedOffset`, so the three random streams don't correlate), and it's now a required part of the save (`Franchise.league`, round-trips through `franchise_json.dart`/`league_json.dart`/`ai_team_roster_json.dart`) rather than something re-derived on the fly.
- **AI roster generation, target composition.** `generateAiRoster` (`roster/generation/`) shapes each team to the Phase 2 target rather than a flat quality curve: one 5-star player (quality center 97, leaning veteran — age 29-34), three 4-star players (quality center 83) with a deliberate young/mid/old age spread (one each in 20-24, 25-29, 30-34), and eight role players (quality center 54, low enough to stay structurally clear of the four-star line most of the time). Which position lands which tier is shuffled per team, so the "best player" isn't always the same position across the league. `generatePlayer` gained optional `minAge`/`maxAge` parameters to support the age-tier targeting (defaults unchanged, so every existing caller is unaffected). Traits are handled separately, team-wide, by `distributeTraits` — see the Phase 1.5 trait-distribution note above. (The star-tier quality center was later retuned from 94 to 97 — see the archetype-drives-stats bullet below.)
- **Shared 12-player position plan.** `kTwelvePlayerPositionPlan` (`roster/generation/roster_position_plan.dart`) factors out the position-coverage list previously private to the starting-roster generator, so both it and the new AI-roster generator stay in sync by construction rather than by convention.
- **A latent contract surfaced and documented.** `createExpansionFranchise`'s `replacedTeamAbbreviation` parameter must actually be one of the 20 teams `drawLeagueTeams` draws for the given `simulationSeed`, not just any `kLeagueTeamPool` member — `League`'s constructor now asserts exactly 19 AI teams, which fails loudly if the two are out of sync. Onboarding already gets this right by construction (it draws the league first, then only offers picks from within that draw); several older direct-unit-test fixtures for `createExpansionFranchise` didn't and had to be corrected once this assert started catching them.
- **Season schedule generator.** `generateSeasonSchedule` (`season/generation/`) builds everything that can be determined before any game is actually played: the 2-game preseason (two independent random inter-conference pairings), the full 28-game regular season (a proper double round-robin within each 10-team conference via the standard "circle method," plus a single round-robin across conferences, greedily packed into the 17-week regular-season window so no team gets more than 2 games in the same week), and the WBL Continental Cup's Round 1 (all 20 teams randomly seeded into 10 games). `ScheduledGame`/`SeasonSchedule` (`season/domain/`) hold the result — deterministic for a given seed, same pattern as `League`. Deliberately **not yet wired into `Franchise`/persistence**: nothing consumes a schedule yet (no simulator, no schedule-viewing screen), so persisting it now would be premature plumbing — unlike `League`, which had `LeagueScreen` as a real, already-built consumer. Continental Cup Rounds 2-5 aren't generated at all yet, since each round's matchups depend on the previous round's actual results (margin-of-victory byes, then a random draw among survivors) — see `0B_Planned.md`.
- **Curated team colors at onboarding.** The GM now picks the new club's colors from `kStarterPalettes`' 6 curated swatches (a random one pre-selected, same "random default, GM-overridable" pattern as the team-to-replace picker) instead of it being silently auto-assigned from `simulationSeed` with no say at all. `createExpansionFranchise` takes the choice as a required `colors` parameter rather than computing it internally.
- **Archetype-drives-stats generation reorder.** `generatePlayer` (`player_generator.dart`) now rolls the archetype *first*, then biases ratings generation to actually fit it, instead of archetype being a cosmetic label picked independently of the rolled stats. A new `_archetypeBias` table (one entry per archetype, smaller magnitudes than the existing position bias — fine-tuning on top, not a second full personality) is combined with the position bias before any per-stat jitter, so a "Sniper" reliably ends up with higher perimeter offense and a "Low Post Monster" with higher interior offense (verified statistically in `player_generator_test.dart`). This surfaced a real reliability bug in AI-roster generation: with the star-tier slot's original quality center of 94 (close to the 99 cap), positive archetype-bias contributions were getting clamped away while negative ones fully applied, net-dragging the 12-stat average below the 90 five-star cutoff in ~4.4% of rosters. Fixed by tightening the star-tier target from quality center 94/spread 3 to 97/spread 2, verified empirically at 0 failures across a 500-roster sample.
- **Match engine, first piece: the shared contest resolver.** `resolveContest`/`contestProbability` (`lib/features/match/engine/contest_resolver.dart`) is the shared skill-check formula every contested in-game action (shots, passes, drives, rebounds, tip-offs) will roll through: a logistic curve centered at 50% when attacker and defender ratings are equal, with tunable `floor`/`ceiling` (neither side is ever a lock, even at a 99-vs-1 mismatch) and `steepness` (how hard a rating gap swings the odds) per action type, instead of every action re-deriving its own formula. Decided 2026-08-05 to build the full possession-based match engine directly rather than a lighter placeholder simulator first — see `0B_Planned.md`'s Phase 2/Phase 3 note.
- **Match engine, second piece: the possession state machine.** `simulatePossession` (`lib/features/match/engine/possession_engine.dart`) runs one possession end to end -- ball handler decision (pass / drive / jump shot, shifting toward a forced shot as the shot clock runs down), pass resolution with a 3-way disrupted-pass outcome (steal, redirect to a random player on the floor, out of bounds), shot resolution (drives use strength+interiorOffense vs strength+interiorDefense, jumpers use agility+perimeterOffense vs agility+perimeterDefense, with a flat rate of jumpers being 3-point attempts since there's no separate three-point rating yet), and rebounding (strength+interiorOffense vs strength+interiorDefense per `PlayerRatings`' own doc comment, offense capped below 50/50 since offensive boards are the exception). Every action costs 1-5 simulated seconds; an offensive rebound resets the shot clock to 14. Returns a `PossessionResult`: an ordered `MatchEvent` log plus the summary a game loop needs (points scored, how it ended, seconds elapsed) -- box-score stats and any future play-by-play text both reduce over that event log rather than being tracked separately. `resolveTipOff` (`tip_off_resolver.dart`) handles the jump ball as its own special case: height dominates (weighted well above strength/interior skill) but never fully decides it. All constants (decision weights, disruption-outcome split, shot floors/ceilings) are initial guesses, explicitly not yet calibrated -- a throwaway sanity check against a real generated roster (5v5, 80 possessions) landed at ~1.0 points/possession and a ~45% scoring rate, both in the right neighborhood for WNBA pace, though the turnover rate ran noticeably higher than real league averages; a proper calibration pass (batch-simulate thousands of games, compare aggregate stats to real WNBA numbers, retune) is still open work.
- **Match engine, third piece: fouls, free throws, and the full game loop.** `simulatePossession` now rolls fouls: a shooting-foul chance on every shot attempt (higher on drives than jumpers, matching real contact rates) sends the shooter to the line -- and-1 on a make, 2 or 3 shots on a miss -- and a disrupted pass has a chance of becoming a reach-in foul, which only sends the ball handler to the line if `defenseInBonus` is true (5+ team fouls that quarter), otherwise it's a dead ball and the offense just keeps the possession. Free throws resolve against a fixed neutral rating via `perimeterOffense` (no dedicated free-throw rating exists). New `PossessionEnd.deadBallStop` covers a foul trip that produced zero points -- distinct from a turnover (the offense didn't lose the ball) and from a defensive rebound (no live rebound off a missed free throw in this engine). `simulateMatch` (`match_engine.dart`) is the new outer loop tying possessions into an actual game: 4 quarters of 600 simulated seconds each, a running home/away score (plus a per-quarter breakdown for a scoreboard), personal and team foul tracking (6 fouls = foul-out, immediately substituted; 5 team fouls = bonus), and automatic substitutions (`substitution_policy.dart`) via a default "best players get the most minutes" ranking -- `targetMinutesFor` assigns `0B_Planned.md`'s reference target-minutes table by rating rank (summing to 200 player-minutes), and `pickOnCourt` picks whichever 5 available players are furthest behind their target each quarter. There's no GM-set minutes ranking to plug in yet, so this default stands in until that UI exists, same "sensible default, later overridable" shape as team colors and the team-to-replace picker. Alternating possession falls out of the engine for free -- every `PossessionEnd` hands the ball to the team that was on defense, so no separate bookkeeping was needed beyond deciding who starts each quarter (strict home/away/home/away alternation keyed off the opening tip, a simplification of the real alternating-possession-arrow rule). Lineups re-pick every 2 simulated minutes (`_substitutionCheckSeconds`), not just at quarter boundaries (foul-outs substitute immediately on top of that). No energy/fatigue model yet (`0B_Planned.md`'s stamina appendix) -- substitutions are target-minutes- and foul-out-driven only.
  **Two calibration findings from a throwaway full-game sanity check, both addressed same-day (2026-08-05):** (1) total game scores ran high (184-239 in one sample, combined ~420 vs. real ~160-170) because average possession length was too short (~2-3 actions, well under a realistic ~24s shot clock's worth of play). Fixed by raising `_passWeight` (0.55 → 0.82, so a possession runs more like 4-5 actions before a shot) and, since more passes per possession meant more chances to get stripped, raising the pass-clean floor/ceiling (0.55/0.95 → 0.90/0.99) to keep the turnover rate from compounding upward; also trimmed the drive/jumper shot-success ceilings down slightly (0.85/0.75 → 0.78/0.65) since efficiency was still running a bit hot after the pacing fix. Re-verified across 20 games: combined score averaged ~201 (down from ~420) and possessions/game landed within ~8% of the ~160 target -- closer, not exact, still real work for the eventual full calibration pass. (2) the bottom of the bench (4-8 target minutes, less than a full quarter) never played, because lineups only re-picked once per quarter. Fixed by adding a periodic recheck every 2 simulated minutes (`_substitutionCheckSeconds`) instead of only at quarter starts; re-verified across 20 games, 0 had any player under 30 seconds of court time.
- **Match engine, fourth piece: overtime, and the season simulator.** `simulateMatch` no longer allows a tied final score -- a season simulator needs a winner out of every game, so the quarter loop now keeps playing 5-minute overtime periods (real WNBA/NBA length) until the tie breaks, extending `MatchResult.homeScoreByQuarter`/`awayScoreByQuarter` past 4 entries when it happens (verified empirically reachable, not just theoretical). `simulateSeason` (`season/generation/season_simulator.dart`) is the new piece tying it all together: runs `simulateMatch` for every `ScheduledGame` in a `SeasonSchedule`, in week order, against one continuing seed, given a roster (12 players) per team abbreviation -- deliberately thin, since the actual game simulation already lives in the match engine. `GameResult` (`season/domain/`) pairs a `ScheduledGame` with its `MatchResult` and exposes the winner/loser abbreviation (plus `winningMargin`, used by the Continental Cup below). `computeStandings` derives a sorted win-pct/point-differential standings table from a list of `GameResult`s, filtering to `GameType.regularSeason` only -- a placeholder ordering, not the real tiebreaker system `0B_Planned.md` still has open. A throwaway full-league sanity check (20 real generated teams, the actual 310-game schedule) simulated the entire season in **207ms** and produced a plausible win-loss spread (6-22 through 19-9) with point differentials broadly tracking records -- directly answers the original "is this processor-efficient" question from a resounding yes. Not yet wired into `Franchise`/persistence or any screen -- like the schedule generator before it, nothing consumes a season result yet.
- **Continental Cup Rounds 2-5.** `continental_cup_generator.dart` picks up where Round 1 (already scheduled) leaves off, one round at a time since each depends on the previous round's actual results: `generateContinentalCupRound2` takes Round 1's 10 winners, gives the 6 biggest margins-of-victory a bye straight to the Quarterfinals, and randomly pairs the other 4 into 2 games -- the only bye in the whole bracket, needed once to turn 10 (not a power of two) into a clean 8. `generateContinentalCupRound3`/`Round4`/`Round5` are the Quarterfinals (8→4), Semifinals (4→2), and Final (2→champion), each just a random-draw pairing of the previous round's winners (plus the Round 2 byes, for Round 3 specifically). All four deterministic for a given seed, all with loud asserts on the expected input shape (e.g., Round 4 asserts it was handed exactly 4 Quarterfinal results) -- same "fail loudly if a caller gets the wiring wrong" posture as `League`'s roster-count assert. A throwaway end-to-end check chained a real 20-team season's actual Round 1 results through every round to a single champion with no manual intervention. Like the season simulator, not wired into anything yet -- these are the round-generation functions a future season loop would call as each round's games get simulated.
- **Postseason bracket.** `postseason_generator.dart` mirrors the real 2022+ WNBA format: `postseasonSeeds` takes the top 8 teams (no conference restriction) off a final `computeStandings` table; `generatePostseasonFirstRound` pairs them in standard 1v8/2v7/3v6/4v5 bracket seeding, each a best-of-3; `generatePostseasonSemifinals` follows a fixed (not reseeded) bracket -- the 1v8 and 4v5 winners meet, as do the 2v7 and 3v6 winners -- each best-of-5; `generatePostseasonFinals` is the 2 survivors' best-of-7 for the championship. The shared `simulateSeries` engine plays real WNBA/NBA home-court patterns (2-1, 2-2-1, 2-2-1-1-1, higher seed hosting the extra games) and stops as soon as a side clinches -- a sweep is genuinely shorter than a full series, not padded out. `SeriesResult` (`season/domain/`) holds the games actually played (each tagged `GameType.postseason` with a 1-3 round number, extending `ScheduledGame`/`GameResult` the same way the Continental Cup's rounds did) and derives the winner from win count vs. `winsNeeded`. Same "not wired into anything yet" posture as the rest of the season-simulation layer -- these are the functions a future end-of-season loop would call once real standings exist to seed off of. One honest gap: `simulateMatch` has no home-court-advantage mechanic at all yet, so who hosts a given playoff game is currently cosmetic, not a real edge -- worth remembering whenever the real calibration pass happens.
- **The draft.** New `lib/features/draft/` feature, resolving `0B_Planned.md`'s last open Draft item. `College`/`kColleges` (`draft/domain/college.dart`) transcribes all 100 fictional schools from `colleges.md` into code (verified programmatically byte-for-byte against the source table, not hand-checked). `generateDraftClass` (`draft_generator.dart`) generates young (20-23), zero-experience prospects in a top-heavy talent shape (2 lottery-caliber, 12 solid, the rest a long fringe tail) -- and, per this session's earlier trait-redesign conversation, this is where `generateTraits` finally gets used for real: most traits now enter the league through incoming prospects, not team-wide roster generation. College assignment is weighted toward `CollegePrestige.premier` schools (`colleges.md`'s "prestige affects exposure" guideline) but never touches a prospect's ratings. `generateDraftOrder` runs a weighted lottery for the 12 non-playoff teams (worse record = more weight, nothing guaranteed) followed by the 8 playoff teams in reverse-standings order -- this project's own resolution of the "lottery/order specifics TBD" note, not a literal replica of the real WNBA's ping-pong-ball process. `simulateDraft` plays out all 3 rounds with a "best player available" AI (no team-needs modeling yet). ## Cross-cutting fixes along the way

- A save-load failure on saves written before archetypes/traits existed (`playerFromJson` used non-nullable casts on fields that didn't exist in older data) was fixed with graceful fallbacks — but going forward, the standing practice for schema churn during this pre-release phase is delete-and-recreate the save rather than defensive parsing; see `0C_Vision_and_Ideas.md`.
- **`generatePlayer` could roll `potential` below `overall`** (a real GM complaint: a 22-year-old with OVR 52 and potential 40) because potential was generated completely independently of the actual rolled ratings. Fixed by computing `overall` from the generated ratings first, then generating potential as `overall + ageSkewedOffset` (never negative) -- young players (≤23) get an 8-35 point gap, tapering through the same age bands `features/training/`'s growth curve already uses, down to a veteran's 0-6. A freshly generated, never-trained player's ceiling should never sit below their current ability.
- **Position display showed the raw camelCase enum name** (`pointGuard`) instead of a real label.
